How Does Skrill Work for Freelancers? Withdrawing to a Bank Account, Explained

Skrill is the rail in my payment stack that most freelancers in supported countries never need — and that freelancers in countries like mine cannot skip. My Skrill account is open and verified in my own name, and it exists for two concrete reasons: Freelancer.com pays out through it, and Adobe Stock pays contributors through it — where the only choices are PayPal or Skrill, and PayPal cannot receive money in Bangladesh. This guide covers how the account works, how to withdraw money from Skrill to a bank account, and where the fees sit. The honest limit, up front: my Skrill balance is $0 and I have not completed a withdrawal yet, because no platform has paid me yet. So the setup below is my own completed work, and the withdrawal walkthrough is the documented process from Skrill’s own interface and help pages — clearly labelled, and due to be replaced by my real receipt when the first payout lands. Published November 2026 · by Tanvir Ahmed.

Key takeaways

  • Skrill is an e-wallet: platforms pay your Skrill balance, and you withdraw from the balance to your own bank account. The wallet is the middle step, not the destination.
  • In my stack it is the payout rail for Freelancer.com withdrawals and Adobe Stock contributor payouts (released from a $25 balance) — Adobe Stock offers only PayPal or Skrill, and PayPal cannot receive in Bangladesh, so Skrill is my only door there.
  • Receiving a payout from a platform into Skrill is typically free. The costs sit at the far end: a bank withdrawal fee and a currency-conversion margin when the balance changes currency. Skrill revises its fees — check the live fee page on your own account before you rely on any figure, including old ones quoted elsewhere.
  • My status, stated plainly: account verified in my own name, bank details ready, balance $0, no withdrawal completed yet. This article documents the setup and the process — it is not a withdrawal receipt, and it will be updated when it becomes one.

What Skrill Is, in One Paragraph

Skrill is a digital wallet run by the Paysafe group, older than most of the freelance economy it serves. You open an account in your own name and country, verify your identity, and the account gives you a balance that other services can pay into. Marketplaces and content platforms that support Skrill send your earnings to that balance; from there you withdraw to a bank account in your own name. It does the same basic job as Payoneer — platform balance first, local bank second — which is exactly why it raises the obvious question of why a freelancer would hold both. The answer is coverage: platforms choose their payout partners, not you, and the sets do not fully overlap.

Where Skrill Actually Matters in My Stack

Two of my income routes run through Skrill, and one of them has no alternative:

  • Adobe Stock — Skrill is the only door. Adobe pays contributors from a $25 balance, and the payout methods it offers are PayPal and Skrill. PayPal does not offer receiving to residents of Bangladesh, which removes half the menu and leaves Skrill as the entire route between my stock portfolio and my bank. My first assets are live on Adobe Stock with earnings at $0 so far — the account is connected and waiting, which is the correct order: payout plumbing should be finished before money arrives, not negotiated after.
  • Freelancer.com — a supported withdrawal rail. Freelancer.com offers Skrill among its withdrawal methods, so my verified account gives that platform a clean exit to my bank alongside its other options. I keep the rail ready even where a choice exists, because withdrawal pricing differs between methods at the moment you actually withdraw — having both verified accounts costs nothing and preserves the choice.

Everywhere else in my stack, other rails do the work: Fiverr pays through Payoneer, my own store pays through Polar as Merchant of Record, and KDP pays by direct deposit. The full comparison is in How Do I Receive Freelance Payments Without PayPal? Every Route I Use, Compared. Skrill’s job is narrower and specific — and on the Adobe Stock route, irreplaceable.

Setting Up: What Verification Actually Asked of Me

Opening the account was the standard e-wallet sequence, done entirely in my own name and country: register with an email address, enter personal details exactly as they appear on my official ID, verify the account by submitting identity documents through Skrill’s verification flow, and add the bank account the withdrawals will go to. Two practical notes from doing it. First, the name on the Skrill account, the name on the ID and the name on the bank account must match — payout accounts in someone else’s name, or registered to another country, are how balances get frozen, and no shortcut around that is worth the risk. Second, verify before the first payout is due, not after: verification queues are nobody’s emergency but yours, and a platform payout sitting behind an unfinished verification is a self-inflicted delay. My account completed this and shows as verified — that, and a $0 balance, is the whole of my Skrill history so far.

How to Withdraw Money From Skrill to a Bank Account (the Documented Process)

What follows is the withdrawal flow as Skrill documents it and as the interface presents it — I have walked to the door of this process setting the account up, but I have not pushed a real balance through it, and I will not write as if I had. The steps:

  • Step 1 — The platform payout lands. You request the payout inside the platform (Adobe Stock from $25; Freelancer.com from its own minimum), choosing Skrill. The money arrives in your Skrill balance in the currency the platform pays.
  • Step 2 — Open Withdraw and choose bank transfer. In your Skrill account, the Withdraw section offers the bank account you linked during setup. A first withdrawal to a new bank account can prompt an extra confirmation of the account details — expect it, and enter them from your bank documents, not from memory.
  • Step 3 — Enter the amount and read the conversion line before confirming. If your balance is in dollars and your bank account is in taka, the withdrawal includes a currency conversion. The screen shows the amount you will receive after the withdrawal fee and the conversion rate — this is the moment to check the real cost, because both deductions happen here, not later.
  • Step 4 — Confirm and wait the stated processing window. Skrill presents bank withdrawals as taking a few working days to reach the account. Until I have a completed withdrawal of my own, treat any exact day-count — mine or anyone’s — as an estimate the platform gives, not a promise.

The Fee Points, Honestly and Without Invented Numbers

Skrill’s fee structure has a shape worth understanding, because the expensive moment is not where beginners look for it. Receiving a payout from a platform into your Skrill balance is typically free — the platforms’ payouts are treated as deposits, and the wallet is happy to fill. The costs sit at the exit: withdrawing to a bank account carries a withdrawal fee, and withdrawing in a different currency than the balance carries a conversion margin on top of the exchange rate. There is a third, quieter charge to know about: wallets of this type apply service fees to accounts left inactive for long periods, so a Skrill account opened “just in case” and forgotten with money inside can leak slowly. I am deliberately not printing precise percentages or flat amounts here: Skrill sets fees by country and method and revises them, and a number copied from any guide — including this site — ages badly. The fee page inside your own Skrill account is the only version that counts, and reading the confirmation screen at Step 3 is the only quote that binds. Bottom Line

For a freelancer in a country PayPal doesn’t serve, Skrill is not a curiosity — it is the second rail that makes routes like Adobe Stock payable at all. Set it up in your own name, verify it early, link your real bank account, and understand before your first balance arrives that the money is only home when it has crossed the last step: the withdrawal, with its fee and its conversion, to your bank. The Bankrate June 2025 baseline is why this plumbing deserves an article of its own — with median side-hustle earnings at $200 a month and only 14% of hustlers above $1,000, the difference between rails is real money at real scales. My rail is built and verified; what it has not yet carried is a dollar. When Adobe Stock or Freelancer.com pays the first one, this page is updated with the amount, the fee, the rate and the dates — the receipt this guide currently, and honestly, lacks.
