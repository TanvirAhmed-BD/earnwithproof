How Do I Get Paid From Amazon KDP in Bangladesh? Royalties, Payoneer and the $100 Rule

If you publish from Bangladesh, Amazon KDP cannot pay you the simple way: Bangladesh is not on KDP’s direct-deposit list, so your royalties travel by wire or through a payment service provider once they reach a threshold of roughly $100 — and they arrive about 60 days after the month in which the sale happened. I have four books live on my own KDP account and my payment route is set up and verified; this guide walks through exactly how to receive Amazon KDP royalties in Bangladesh, from the account settings to the wait. Published November 2026 · by Tanvir Ahmed.

Key takeaways

  • Bangladesh is not on Amazon KDP’s direct-deposit (EFT) country list, so there is no “enter your local bank account” option — payment goes by wire transfer or a payment service provider such as Payoneer.
  • Non-EFT payments carry a threshold of roughly $100 (per marketplace currency): below it, royalties simply accumulate in your KDP account until they cross it.
  • KDP pays about 60 days after the end of the sale month, per marketplace — a book sold in January is paid at the end of March, and only once the threshold for that marketplace is met.

Why Bangladesh Is a Special Case for KDP Payments

Most KDP guides are written by US, UK or EU authors, and for them the payment chapter is one line long: Amazon deposits royalties straight into a bank account every month. That works because their countries are on Amazon’s direct-deposit list. Bangladesh is not on that list — and neither are many other countries where independent authors are quietly publishing. If you skip the payment setup, KDP does not chase you; your royalties just sit inside your account, reported in the dashboard, with nowhere to go.

This matters before your first sale, not after. When I set up my own account, I treated the payment and tax screens as part of publishing the first book, not as admin to do later. My live catalogue on my own account currently includes a dinosaur activity book for kids (priced $9.99) and a large-print Christmas word search (133 pages, $11.99), with a four-volume word search series, ten adult nonfiction titles and six parent guides built and queued behind them. My wife publishes children’s activity books on her own separate KDP account, with her own legal, tax and payment details — Amazon’s two-account reality for couples is that the accounts never mix, never carry the same book, and each one’s royalties flow to its own owner. Everything below is about my account and my route.

The Routes KDP Actually Offers Where Direct Deposit Doesn’t Exist

When you open the payment section of a KDP account registered in Bangladesh, the choices narrow to two practical routes:

  • Wire transfer. Amazon wires the money in US dollars once your accrued royalties for a marketplace reach the wire threshold — roughly $100 equivalent. Your bank’s own receiving fees and the exchange rate it applies come out of what you ultimately see in taka.
  • A payment service provider (PSP) receiving account. Services such as Payoneer give you receiving details that act like a local account in the US or another supported country. Royalties land there, and you withdraw from the PSP to your Bangladeshi bank at a rate and fee you can actually see before confirming.

My setup uses the second route. My Payoneer account is verified in my own name, and it is the same account already linked to my Fiverr seller profile, so every income stream I am building drains into one place I control. The practical advantages are visibility and control: I can see the balance, choose when to withdraw, and see the conversion before it happens, instead of a wire arriving at whatever rate the receiving bank decides that day. The $100 Rule and the 60-Day Wait, Explained

Two numbers govern KDP money for authors outside the direct-deposit list, and together they explain why new authors think KDP “isn’t paying them” when it is simply queuing:

The threshold. Royalties accrue per marketplace (Amazon.com, Amazon.co.uk, and so on) and per currency. Until a marketplace’s pot reaches about $100 equivalent, no payment is released from it. Ten small sales scattered across five stores can mean five pots, none of them ripe. This is the single most misunderstood part of how to receive Amazon KDP royalties in Bangladesh and other non-EFT countries: nothing is lost, but nothing moves early, either.

The delay. KDP pays roughly 60 days after the end of the month in which a sale occurred. A sale on 10 January belongs to the January royalty month and is scheduled for payment around the end of March — assuming its marketplace pot has crossed the threshold by then. Paperback and ebook royalties follow the same calendar. Build your expectations around that lag: KDP is a slow, compounding income stream, not a weekly wage.

Set against the wider reality of side income, that patience is normal. Bankrate’s June 2025 survey of 2,616 US adults found side-hustle participation had fallen to 27%, median earnings were $200 a month, and only 14% of side hustlers earned over $1,000 a month. A royalty route that pays quarterly-sized, threshold-gated amounts is unglamorous — and it is also exactly how most real publishing income behaves at the start.

The Tax Interview: The Step That Decides Your Withholding

Before KDP releases anything, it requires a tax interview. For a non-US author this is the W-8BEN declaration: you state your country of tax residence and claim the treaty position between your country and the US. Skip it or leave it incomplete and Amazon applies the default 30% US withholding to your royalties. Complete it properly, and the US–Bangladesh treaty rate applies to book royalties instead. It is a short form, but two fields trip beginners regularly: your name must match your official documents exactly, and the foreign tax identification number question has a specific “not legally required” route for individuals who genuinely do not have one. Bottom Line

If you are a Bangladeshi author, do not let the missing direct-deposit option put you off KDP — but do set up the alternative before you publish: a verified payment service provider account, the tax interview completed, and a realistic expectation built around the roughly-$100 threshold and the 60-day payment lag. Your royalties are never lost for being small; they wait. Your job is to make sure that when a marketplace pot crosses the line, there is a working road for the money to travel — all the way to your own Bangladeshi bank account, in your own name.
