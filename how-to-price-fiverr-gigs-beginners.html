What Should I Charge on Fiverr? Pricing Your First Gig Without Racing to the Bottom

How to price Fiverr gigs for beginners usually gets answered with either “start at $5” or “charge your worth” — both useless without numbers. So here are mine: the exact three-tier ladders on my four live Fiverr gigs, the job each tier is doing, and the reasoning behind every figure. One disclosure up front, because it shapes everything below: my Fiverr orders so far are zero. This is a pre-first-order pricing strategy, written before the data — and this page gets updated with real order numbers the moment they exist. Published November 2026 · by Tanvir Ahmed.

Key takeaways

  • My real ladders: captions $15/$35/$55 · ATS resume $25/$45/$70 · KDP formatting $30/$60/$100 · one-page websites $55/$95/$145. No $5 tier anywhere.
  • Each tier has a job: the Basic package is priced to win the first orders and reviews, the Standard package is the anchor most buyers are meant to choose, and Premium exists so big jobs are priced honestly instead of being squeezed into a small package.
  • Price backwards from what you keep: Fiverr takes 20% before anything else happens, so a $15 package is a $12 job. Set the sticker after you know the waterfall, not before.
  • This strategy is untested by orders — gigs live, zero sales. When the first orders land, the prices and this article both get reviewed against real conversion data.

My Four Price Ladders, Exactly as Published

These are the live packages on my four gigs today — the same figures any buyer sees:

  • Social media captions — Basic $15 (a small starter set of captions) · Standard $35 (a larger set with a posting plan) · Premium $55 (a full month of captions with the extras — hooks, hashtags and scheduling notes — layered in).
  • ATS resume, cover letter and LinkedIn — Basic $25 (resume only) · Standard $45 (resume + cover letter) · Premium $70 (the full package, LinkedIn profile included).
  • KDP book formatting — Basic $30 (a short book, print-ready interior) · Standard $60 (longer book, full front matter and checks) · Premium $100 (the complete job with the extras a publisher actually needs).
  • One-page business websites — Basic $55 (one page, your content placed and styled) · Standard $95 (one page plus the sections and polish most businesses ask for) · Premium $145 (the full build with the premium extras).

Two things are deliberate across all four. First, the tiers differ by scope, not by gratitude — each step up adds defined deliverables, which is also how the descriptions present them; the structure behind those descriptions is broken down in How Do I Write a Fiverr Gig Description That Converts? (With My Before-and-After). Second, the spread between Basic and Premium is roughly 3× — wide enough that a serious buyer self-selects upward, narrow enough that Premium never looks like a joke price.

How to Price Fiverr Gigs for Beginners: The Three Jobs Each Tier Does

A beginner’s ladder is not a smaller version of an established seller’s ladder. Before the first review exists, each tier is doing a different job than it will do later:

  • Basic is the review engine. It is priced at the lowest figure I can deliver properly — small scope, fast turnaround, no corners cut. Its purpose is not day-one profit; it is to convert a cautious first buyer into a completed order and a public review, which is the asset a new account actually lacks. Priced for reviews does not mean priced at nothing: $15 is the floor because below it the work stops being worth doing well, and badly done cheap work produces the reviews that end accounts.
  • Standard is the anchor. Most buyers who compare three packages pick the middle when the middle is clearly the complete version of the job. So Standard is where the real margin is intended to live: full deliverable, fair price, no upsell needed. When you build your own ladder, price the tier you actually want to sell first — then set Basic below it and Premium above it.
  • Premium is scope protection. Without a top tier, the buyer with the 300-page book or the rush deadline tries to buy the $30 package and negotiate the rest in chat. Premium gives that work an honest home — and it quietly makes Standard look reasonable, which is the anchor effect doing its job.

Why I Didn’t Start at $5

The $5 start is inherited advice from a Fiverr that no longer exists, and the arithmetic alone argues against it: Fiverr keeps 20%, so a $5 order pays $4 before withdrawal costs — a price at which careful work is impossible and careless work is guaranteed. Three further problems matter more than the maths. A $5 gig signals “desperate” to exactly the buyers who check quality signals, and the orders it does attract are disproportionately the demanding, dispute-prone kind. Raising prices later is harder than it sounds — the gig’s early history anchors what buyers expect from it. And the premise underneath $5 pricing — that a seller from Bangladesh must be the cheapest option in the search results — is simply false: buyers compare deliverables, samples and reviews, not flags. My samples are my own books, my own store and my own channel; the price has to be consistent with that proof, or the proof stops being believable.

Price Backwards From What You Actually Keep

One fee fact belongs in every pricing decision, and it is the only one I will repeat here: Fiverr takes 20% of every order, so $15/$35/$55 is really $12/$28/$44 before the money moves anywhere else. The full waterfall — the 20%, the Payoneer withdrawal fee and the conversion spread that turn a $100 order into roughly $73–74 in a Bangladeshi bank account — is worked out fee by fee in How Much Does Fiverr Really Take? A $100 Order Worked Out Fee by Fee, and I won’t re-derive it here. The pricing lesson is the order of operations: decide what a job must net you to be worth an hour of your life, then set the sticker price that survives the waterfall — never pick a round number first and discover the net later.

The Honest Status: A Strategy, Not a Result

Everything above is reasoning, not evidence. My gigs are live, my impressions are real — and as I documented in Why Is My Fiverr Gig Getting Impressions But No Orders? (And What I Changed), impressions are not orders. It is entirely possible that one of these ladders is priced wrong for its market, and the honest way to find out is the way this site handles every claim: wait for the data, then publish it. The Bankrate June 2025 baseline keeps the expectation calibrated — median side-hustle earnings are $200 a month and only 14% of side hustlers clear $1,000 — so a pricing strategy is a hypothesis inside a slow game, not a lever that prints money. My review triggers are set in advance: after the first orders, if buyers cluster on Basic, the anchor is wrong; after the first reviews exist, Basic prices rise, because the review engine has done its job. When either happens, this page is updated with the dates and the numbers.

Bottom Line

Price your first gig like a system, not a guess: a Basic tier low enough to earn the first reviews but never below the value of doing the work properly, a Standard tier carrying the real margin, and a Premium tier that gives big jobs an honest price. Count the platform’s 20% before you set a single figure, refuse the $5 race — it pays $4 and costs you positioning you cannot buy back — and treat every price as provisional until your own order data replaces your reasoning. Mine is published above, orders and all: currently, zero orders and all.
