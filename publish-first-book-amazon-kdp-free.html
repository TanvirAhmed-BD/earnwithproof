How Do I Publish My First Book on Amazon KDP for Free? (Step by Step, With Canva)

Publishing on Amazon KDP genuinely costs $0: no upload fee, no printing inventory, no ISBN purchase required. The cost is your time and your care at about ten specific steps. This is the exact flow I used to put my own books live — manuscript, a cover built in the free version of Canva, the upload, the AI-disclosure questions, pricing, and the review wait — including one mistake of mine you can skip. Published November 2026 · by Tanvir Ahmed.

Key takeaways

  • KDP charges nothing to publish: you upload a manuscript and cover, Amazon prints or delivers per order, and printing cost comes out of the paperback royalty — never out of your pocket upfront.
  • The whole free toolchain works: a word processor for the manuscript, Kindle Create for formatting polish, and free Canva (at the right dimensions, exported as PDF) for the cover.
  • The steps beginners rush are the expensive ones in time: the tax interview, the seven keyword fields, the AI-content disclosure questions, and the online previewer check.
  • My mistake to avoid: thin books cannot carry spine text. A cover that tries will draw a KDP quality flag — leave the spine blank on slim books.

What “Free” Actually Covers — and What It Doesn’t

How do I publish my first book on Amazon KDP for free? Start by knowing precisely which parts are free. Creating the KDP account: free. Uploading an ebook or paperback: free. The ISBN for a paperback: free, if you accept the Amazon-assigned ISBN (using your own is optional and is the paid route in some countries). Distribution to Amazon’s stores: free. What is not free is anything outside the upload itself — paid editing, paid cover design, advertising, and author proof copies (you pay the print cost plus shipping for those). Everything in this guide uses only the free column: it is the exact stack behind my own live books, which include a dinosaur activity book and a large-print Christmas word search on my account, with more titles built and queued behind them.

Step 1: Create the KDP Account and Finish the Tax Interview First

Sign up at KDP with your Amazon account, then — before touching a book file — complete two things in account settings: your bank/payment details and the tax interview. Non-US authors complete a short form (in practice, the W-8BEN declaration) stating your tax residence; skipping it leaves your royalties subject to the default 30% US withholding, and KDP will nag you at every upload until it is done. Do it once, on day one. How the money eventually reaches an author outside the US — including the Payoneer route and the roughly-$100 threshold that applies where direct deposit is unavailable, as it is in Bangladesh — is covered in How Do I Get Paid From Amazon KDP in Bangladesh? Royalties, Payoneer and the $100 Rule.

Step 2: Prepare the Manuscript With Free Tools

Write and assemble the book in whatever word processor you already have — it costs nothing and KDP accepts DOC/DOCX for ebooks and a print-ready PDF for paperbacks. Three habits save you later: use real heading styles for chapter titles (they become your table of contents), keep images at the highest resolution you have, and build the book in the structure readers expect — copyright page, table of contents, introduction, content, conclusion. For paperbacks, work to your final trim size from the start rather than reformatting at the end; KDP’s free Kindle Create tool can polish an ebook file, and for paperbacks a correctly-sized PDF is the deliverable. The detailed free formatting workflow — trim sizes, margins, and the pre-upload checks that catch errors before Amazon does — is its own guide: How Do I Format a KDP Book Without Paid Software? My Free Workflow.

Step 3: Build the Cover in Free Canva, at KDP’s Dimensions

Canva’s free tier is enough for a respectable cover if you respect two rules. Rule one: exact size. For an ebook, upload a JPEG/TIFF at 2560 × 1600 pixels. For a paperback, the cover is a single wrap-around PDF (back cover + spine + front cover) whose total width depends on your page count and paper type — KDP’s cover calculator gives the exact dimensions; build the Canva design at those dimensions and export as PDF Print, not PNG. Rule two: safe zones. Keep all text away from the trim edges and, critically, respect the spine: a paperback needs a minimum page count before spine text is allowed at all, and slim books must ship with a blank spine.

That second rule is my documented mistake. One of my own thin titles was built with text running down a very slim spine — the kind of cover that looks fine on screen and fails in print reality, where a fraction of a millimetre of shift makes spine text crooked or clipped. KDP flags exactly this. The fix costs nothing: on thin books, design the spine as plain colour and put your title and name on the front, where there is room to be read. Check the spine early, not at the upload screen.

Step 4: The Book Details Page — Title, Keywords, Categories

Creating the title in KDP walks through three screens. On the first (book details), the fields that matter most are the ones beginners treat as paperwork: the title and subtitle (the subtitle is prime space for the phrase a buyer would actually search), the description (write it like a sales page, because it is one — who the book is for, what is inside, why this one), seven keyword fields (use all seven; each accepts a phrase, not just a word — think like a buyer typing into Amazon’s search bar), and categories (choose the most specific ones your book honestly fits). Which niche deserves this effort in the first place is a research question — the under-1,000-results test I use before committing to a topic is in How Do I Find a KDP Niche That Isn’t Saturated? The Under-1,000-Results Test I Use. Get the details page right here and you will not be editing metadata at midnight later.

Step 5: Upload, Answer the AI-Disclosure Questions Honestly

On the content screen you upload the manuscript and cover, set digital rights (your own copyright, worldwide), and answer KDP’s AI-content disclosure questions. The rule is simple and non-optional: if AI generated text, images or translations in the book — even if you edited them afterwards — you disclose it; purely AI-assisted work (your own writing, spell-checked or edited with AI tools) does not require disclosure. Answer for what is actually in the file. Then run the online previewer and page through the whole book. The previewer is unglamorous and it is the highest-value ten minutes in the process: it shows trim errors, cut-off text, low-resolution images and blank pages exactly as a buyer would receive them. Every error you catch here is a one-star review that never happens.

Step 6: Pricing, Royalties and the Review Wait

On the final screen you set territories and price. For ebooks, the royalty choice is the headline: 70% on ebooks priced between $2.99 and $9.99 (minus a small delivery fee), 35% outside that band. For paperbacks there is no percentage choice — you receive 60% of the list price minus the printing cost, which is why a paperback’s price floor is set by its page count. My own activity books sit at $9.99–$11.99, priced against comparable titles, not against hope. Royalties, when they come, are paid about 60 days after the end of the month in which the sale occurred — a January sale is late-March money, a delay every KDP budget has to respect.

Then you press publish, and you wait. Amazon states a review window of up to 72 hours for most books, and my own titles cleared within that standard window — the book appears first as “In Review,” then flips to “Live,” and the product page follows shortly after. Do not use the review days to second-guess the price; use them to prepare the book’s announcement and your next title. And keep the expectations baseline where this site always keeps it: Bankrate’s June 2025 survey found median side-hustle earnings of $200/month across all methods, and only 14% of side hustlers above $1,000/month. A first book is the start of a catalogue, not a lottery ticket — most KDP authors who earn steadily do it across many titles, published one honest book at a time.

Bottom Line

Free on KDP is real: account, upload, ISBN and distribution all cost $0, and a word processor plus free Canva covers the production. The steps that decide whether the free book sells are the unglamorous ones — tax interview done first, a cover built to exact dimensions with a legal spine, seven honest keyword phrases, the AI disclosure answered truthfully, and ten patient minutes in the previewer. Do those, and your first book is live inside a week, built entirely from tools that were already on your computer.
