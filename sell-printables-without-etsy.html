How Can I Sell Printables Without Etsy? My Own-Store Route, Step by Step

Every guide on how to sell printables without Etsy assumes you could use Etsy and chose not to. Mine starts from the harder fact: I am a seller in Bangladesh, and Etsy will not take me at all. Since 2021 every Etsy seller must be paid through Etsy Payments, and Bangladesh is not on the list of countries eligible for it — no Etsy Payments, no shop, no exceptions. So the route below is the one I actually built: a free storefront I control, a Merchant of Record handling the checkout, and the same printable work also published as print books on Amazon KDP. One status line before the steps: the store is live, the printables are listed, and sales so far are zero — this is a route guide, not a results story. Published November 2026 · by Tanvir Ahmed.

Key takeaways

  • Etsy is closed to sellers in Bangladesh — not difficult, closed. Etsy Payments is mandatory for every shop and Bangladesh is not an eligible country. Check your own country’s eligibility on any marketplace before you build a product for it.
  • My route: a free GitHub Pages storefront + Polar as Merchant of Record at checkout. Polar charges the buyer’s card, handles the sales tax and fraud screening, and pays my Bangladeshi bank — no PayPal, no Stripe account of my own.
  • The same planners and trackers also earn a second shelf as print books on Amazon KDP, where Amazon handles the shop, the printing and the customer, and pays royalties by direct deposit.
  • The honest trade-off: Etsy brings built-in shopper traffic and an own store brings none. What you keep is control — nobody can close the route out from under you — and what it costs you is doing your own traffic work.

Why Etsy Is Closed to Me (and the Check Everyone Should Run First)

Printables are one of the most-searched digital products on the internet — the head term “printables” draws around 165,000 searches a month — and Etsy is where most of that demand is taught to shop. So Etsy was my first plan too, right up until I read the payout rules instead of the success stories. Etsy closed enrolment in its old payment arrangements years ago; today a shop is paid only through Etsy Payments, and the eligibility list for Etsy Payments does not include Bangladesh. That is the whole story. There is no application to appeal, no setting to change, and the workarounds people sell — a shop registered through another country, a relative’s details — put the account in breach of Etsy’s terms and risk the balance inside it. I do not build on ground that can be confiscated.

The lesson generalises well beyond Bangladesh, and it is the first step of this guide: before you make a single product for any marketplace, verify that the marketplace can actually pay a seller in your country, into your own bank, in your own name. It takes ten minutes on the platform’s official help pages and it decides whether you are building a business or decorating someone else’s. Gumroad failed the same test for me — its bank payouts do not reach Bangladesh, though it works fine for sellers in many other countries, so check your own eligibility rather than copying my verdict. Etsy failed it too. The two routes below are the ones that passed.

How to Sell Printables Without Etsy: The Route I Actually Built

My setup has four parts, and the total cost to build it was $0:

  • Step 1 — Make the printable. My planners and trackers are designed in Canva — budget trackers, planners and family organisers, built for a specific buyer with a specific recurring problem rather than as generic decoration. The design discipline is the same one I use for templates generally, covered in How Do I Sell Canva Templates? What Buyers Actually Search For: start from what buyers search for, then narrow to a niche one person can serve well.
  • Step 2 — Put up a storefront you own. My store is a simple static website hosted free on GitHub Pages. No monthly platform fee, no percentage of every sale going to a marketplace, and — the part that matters most to me — no platform that can suspend the shop overnight. It is plain HTML: a product grid, an honest description and price on each item, and a buy button.
  • Step 3 — Let a Merchant of Record run the checkout. The buy buttons point to Polar. Polar is the legal seller of record: it charges the buyer’s card, calculates and remits the sales tax and VAT that digital products attract across borders, screens for fraud, delivers the download, and pays me out to my Bangladeshi bank account. Its published fee is 5% plus $0.50 per sale — the full reasoning behind choosing it is in How Can I Sell Digital Products Without PayPal or Stripe? My Polar Merchant-of-Record Setup From Bangladesh. This is the step that replaces Etsy: Etsy bundles the shop, the checkout and the tax handling; off Etsy, you assemble those pieces yourself, and a Merchant of Record is the piece most beginners don’t know exists.
  • Step 4 — Bring your own traffic. Etsy’s fee buys you a place where shoppers already are. An own store has no shoppers in it. My traffic plan is unglamorous: guides like this one, Pinterest pins pointing at the products, links from my gig pages and profiles, and an email list collected with a free sample. Slow, but every visitor arrives on property I own.

The Second Shelf: The Same Work as KDP Print Books

A planner that works as a PDF download usually also works as a paperback, and that second format plugs into the one marketplace that both accepts Bangladeshi sellers and brings its own traffic: Amazon, through Kindle Direct Publishing. My printable-style work — planners, journals, trackers and activity books — is published on KDP as print books: Amazon lists them, prints each copy when it is ordered, ships it, handles the customer, and pays royalties by direct deposit to my bank. There is no inventory and no shop to run. It is not the same product wearing a new cover — a paperback needs proper print formatting, margins and a cover built for a physical spine — but the underlying content, the niche research and the design work are shared, so one idea earns two listings on two completely independent routes. If one route stalls, the other is untouched.

What I Gave Up, Stated Plainly

Honesty about the trade-off matters more than the steps. Etsy’s published costs — a $0.20 listing fee and a 6.5% transaction fee, plus payment processing — buy real things: millions of existing shoppers, search traffic you do not have to earn, and a trust signal with buyers who have never heard of you. My route replaces the transaction percentage with Polar’s 5% + $0.50 and the hosting with $0, but the traffic line in the ledger is not cheaper — it is mine to produce, in hours instead of fees. And the starting numbers are what they are: my store is live with its products listed, and it has made no sales yet. The Bankrate June 2025 baseline keeps that in proportion — median side-hustle earnings are $200 a month and only 14% of side hustlers earn over $1,000 — the early game is building routes that are legally yours and cannot be switched off, then feeding them traffic. A closed Etsy door forced that design on me; I now think it is the right design anyway.

Bottom Line

You sell printables without Etsy the way you sell anything without a marketplace: own the storefront, rent the checkout from a Merchant of Record, and put the same work on a second shelf — KDP print — where a marketplace does accept you. But run the eligibility check first, on every platform, for your own country: whether the platform can pay you is not a detail to discover after the products are made. If Etsy is open to you, this route still works as the shop you control alongside it; if it is closed to you, as it is to me, this route is not a consolation prize — it is the whole shop, and it cannot be taken away. When the first sale lands, this page gets its receipt.
