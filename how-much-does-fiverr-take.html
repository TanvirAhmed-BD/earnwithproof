How Much Does Fiverr Really Take? A $100 Order Worked Out Fee by Fee

Short answer: on a $100 order, Fiverr’s 20% commission leaves you $80 — and by the time that money has cleared, been withdrawn through Payoneer and converted into taka, roughly $73–$74 of the original $100 is what actually reaches you. This is a worked example from published fee schedules, not my own receipt: my gigs are live but my first order has not landed yet, and the day it does, this page gets my real numbers. Published November 2026 · by Tanvir Ahmed.

Key takeaways

  • Fiverr keeps 20% of the order price (including tips): a $100 order credits you $80, not $100.
  • New sellers wait 14 days for earnings to clear before anything can be withdrawn.
  • The Payoneer withdrawal adds a flat $3 fee, and currency conversion into taka costs roughly another 2–3% — worked example: $100 → $80 → $77 → about $73.72 in value by the time it lands.
  • Total leakage on this route is about 26% — which is exactly why your gig price has to be set with the fees already subtracted, not discovered afterwards.

Step 1: Fiverr’s 20% Commission — $100 Becomes $80

How much does Fiverr take from a $100 order? The first and largest cut is the platform commission: Fiverr keeps 20% of everything a buyer pays you, including the price of gig extras and any tip. The maths is simple and non-negotiable:

$100 order × 20% = $20 to Fiverr. Your credited earnings: $80.

There is no tier where this improves with volume, no way to pass the fee to the buyer, and no cheaper in-platform alternative — 80% is the seller’s share at every level. This is also why comparing Fiverr prices with off-platform prices is misleading: a $100 Fiverr gig and a $100 direct invoice are two different incomes, $20 apart, before anything else happens. Whatever you think of the cut, it buys the marketplace itself — the buyers, the payment processing, the dispute system. The useful response is not resentment; it is pricing that already knows the number, which I work through in What Should I Charge on Fiverr? Pricing Your First Gig Without Racing to the Bottom.

Step 2: The Clearing Period — Your $80 Is Frozen First

The $80 does not become withdrawable when the buyer accepts delivery. Fiverr holds earnings in a “pending clearance” state — 14 days for most sellers (longer in some cases, shorter for top-rated seller programmes). During clearance the money is visible in your balance but untouchable: it is the platform’s protection window against chargebacks and disputes.

Clearance is not a fee, but it is a real cost to plan around: a $100 order completed today is cash in your bank roughly two-plus weeks from today at the earliest, once withdrawal and bank processing time are added. New sellers who price a gig, deliver it, and immediately count the money in their monthly budget learn this the awkward way. My own routine treats cleared balance — never pending balance — as the first moment money exists.

Step 3: The Withdrawal — Payoneer Takes $3, Leaving $77

Once cleared, the $80 can leave Fiverr. For a seller in Bangladesh there is no PayPal route (PayPal does not operate here), so my account uses the route I have actually set up and verified: Fiverr → Payoneer. The Fiverr-to-Payoneer withdrawal carries a flat fee of $3 per withdrawal:

$80 − $3 withdrawal fee = $77 arriving in Payoneer.

The flat fee carries a practical lesson: withdrawing small amounts often is expensive in percentage terms. A $3 fee on a $30 withdrawal is 10%; the same $3 on a $300 withdrawal is 1%. Sellers outside the US generally do better letting cleared earnings accumulate and withdrawing in fewer, larger moves — unless they need the cash, in which case the fee is simply the price of speed. The full route, from cleared balance to a Bangladeshi bank account and bKash, is walked through step by step in How Do I Withdraw My Fiverr Earnings in Bangladesh Without PayPal? (Payoneer to Bank/bKash, Step by Step). Payoneer withdrawal to a local bank in local currency can be free or low-cost depending on the corridor, but the money still has to change currency — which is the last cut.

Step 4: Currency Conversion — $77 Becomes About $73.72 in Taka Value

Unless you hold and spend US dollars, your $77 must be converted into taka, and conversion is never at the headline exchange rate you see on Google. Payoneer’s conversion applies a margin — commonly around 2–3% below the mid-market rate once the small conversion charge is included (the exact percentage is shown to you at the moment of withdrawal, and you should always read it there rather than trusting any article, including this one).

In this worked example the conversion step is costed at roughly $3.28 in total value — about $1 as a conversion charge plus the exchange-rate margin — landing at:

$77 − ~$3.28 conversion cost ≈ $73.72 in taka reaching your bank or bKash.

The taka figure itself moves daily with the exchange rate, which is why this article quotes the dollar-equivalent: the honest, checkable claim is that on this route, a $100 order delivers roughly 73–74 cents on the dollar to a Bangladeshi seller’s local account. Sellers in the US skip most of step 4 (and can withdraw to a bank with different fees), which is why “how much does Fiverr take?” has a different honest answer depending on where you live — and why the no-PayPal routes matter enough to compare properly, as I do in How Do I Receive Freelance Payments Without PayPal? The Full Ledger, and What It Means

Here is the complete worked example in one place:

  • Buyer pays: $100.00
  • Fiverr commission (20%): −$20.00 → you are credited $80.00
  • Clearing: 14 days, $0 fee, but the money is frozen
  • Payoneer withdrawal fee: −$3.00 → $77.00 in Payoneer
  • Conversion to taka (charge + rate margin, ~3% in this example): −~$3.28 → ~$73.72 in local value

Three conclusions follow. First, price backwards. If you need $50 in your bank for a piece of work, a $50 gig price does not get you there — you need roughly a $68 price on this route. Second, batch withdrawals. The flat $3 punishes small, frequent withdrawals. Third, keep perspective. Bankrate’s June 2025 survey (2,616 US adults) put median side-hustle earnings at $200/month, with only 14% of side hustlers earning over $1,000/month; for most beginners the binding constraint is getting orders at all, not the fee stack. The fees are worth understanding precisely because early earnings are small — at $200 a month, a careless 26% leak is $52 you did the work for and never saw.

And the standing promise of this site applies here in full: this example is built from published fee schedules because my own first order — and therefore my own first withdrawal — has not happened yet. When it does, this page is updated with the real receipt: dates, fees and the actual taka figure, whatever they turn out to be.

Bottom Line

On a $100 Fiverr order, expect about $80 after the platform’s cut and roughly $73–74 in hand if you withdraw through Payoneer and convert to taka: a total cost of about 26–27%, plus a two-week wait. That is not a scandal and not a secret — it is simply the real price of the route, and it belongs in your pricing from day one rather than in your surprises.
